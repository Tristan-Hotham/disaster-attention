## Summary for a cover letter (150 words)

Disasters are classic focusing events, yet we know little about how much attention legislatures give the places where they happen. This article follows 64 localised UK disasters from 1910 to 2020 through monthly counts of House of Commons contributions naming the place, with a design pre-specified in a written plan. Attention jumps to 10 times its usual level in the month of a disaster; the median disaster accumulates half of its five-year excess within 8.5 months, though events followed by inquiries hold attention for years. Attention rises with the death toll only weakly and far less than proportionally (elasticity 0.42; 0.52 once 22 disasters found later are added), and the association is not robust. A London premium depends on two events, and there is no clear evidence of neglect of the devolved nations or mining communities. It also shows how lists of remembered disasters and search terms shape such conclusions.
